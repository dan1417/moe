## What is good (keep it)
- **Validator-parity scorer** (`src/parity`): reproduces live verdicts to ~1e-5, document masking, all four
  competitions, loss cache, pipelined replicas (~57 seq/s on 8× B300).
- **Merge machinery**: hidden-permutation recovery into one basis, task vectors, every merge method, a fast writer.
- **Trainer** (`scripts/train/cpt_fsdp.py`): grouped-GEMM experts, validator document masking (checked against the
  MiMo forward), fp32 masters, 8-bit Adam offloaded, EMA, 15-second asynchronous checkpoint writes.
- **The winning pattern**: keep a training lineage of our own, and merge its delta onto every new king fast.

## What went wrong
1. **Training was too timid to move the model.** At lr 1.5e-6–3e-6 with EMA 0.96–0.98 and layers 0–15 frozen, most
   fp32 updates never crossed a bf16 rounding step. 2.5–9 % of weights changed (relative change 3e-5–2e-4); the
   winners' steps change 40–88 % (relative 2.5e-3–1.3e-2). MATH validation moved −3e-5…−9e-5 in 50–60 steps, and three MATH
   runs plateaued at +0.0004 vs MA2.
2. **Probe and abandon.** Higher LRs were judged after 20–50 steps at constant LR, right in the re-warmup bump and
   before any decay, then dropped. Every switch restarted from a bf16 checkpoint with fresh Adam moments (another bump).
3. **Lossy resumes, and a retention bug.** A crash resumed from the EMA bf16 checkpoint with a fresh optimizer.
   `ckpt_retention.sh` sorted `step-100 < step-60` for run names containing a dash, deleted the two newest checkpoints,
   and the 10:27 crash resumed 40 steps back (~2.7 h of 8 GPUs).
4. **Biased local verdicts since Oct 12.** The installed validator package had been hand-edited (outside git; files dated Oct 12 15:59–17:35,
   copies + diffs vs the 14a9bb2 pin in `/data/teutonic/logs/evidence_validator_handedit_20261012/`) to
   drop every long document whose shard was not on disk. The cached samples held TEXT 0, CODE 0.7M, MATH 1.7M and
   MAIN 4.7M of the ~6M document tokens (~9 % of the score). Every screen from then on was window-only or
   window-heavy.
5. **The merge campaign optimized noise.** 161+ candidates on 1 200 sequences (SE ≈ 5e-5–1e-4); the "best" of each
   competition (+0.0001–0.00025) was inflated by 2–3 SE. The same sample A was reused for selection, confirmation and
   training screens. Cached scores were keyed by labels that did not encode the recipe's endpoints, so an anchor
   change reused scores of different models. Days of GPU time went in for ~0 real gain.
6. **Fragile glue.** Six layers of scripts appended flags (argparse last-wins), environment variables leaked into the
   watchers, kings silently fell back to a stale default (`K43`) behind `|| true`, a dead watcher left the trainer
   paused forever, and `download_kings` retried 22 incompatible old kings every 5 minutes (5 TB of useless downloads).

## How to get on top (in order of expected value)
1. **Ride every new king within hours** (that is how all three crowns were won). Always have a training lineage
   running, so that each new king meets a fresh delta of ours that it does not contain. → `bash train.sh <comp>`
   (autopilot) does this by itself: on a new king it stops, runs merge mode, confirms on independent samples, stages,
   and continues.
2. **Make training move the weights**: higher LR (5e-6 base, adaptive ×0.3–×2.4), exact resume (no reset ever), long
   uninterrupted runs, layers 8–47 + embeddings + lm_head trainable, judged on the EMA trend over ≥60 steps.
3. **Screen everything against everything.** Each checkpoint on all four competitions: a MATH lineage can win CODE.
   The specialist bars (0.002) are 1.5× easier than MAIN; CODE's king won by only +0.0022 and three challengers
   missed it by < 0.0001 → CODE is the softest target right now.
4. **Throughput is the next lever.** 41.6k tok/s on 8× B300 (measured) is roughly 8–12 % MFU (an
   estimate: 6 × ~7B active params × tok/s vs the dense BF16 peak; not profiled). 1.5–2× looks reachable (FSDP2 + compile of the
   MoE block, selective activation checkpointing with 275 GB HBM, larger micro-batches, fused CE). More tokens per day
   means a bigger delta of ours at every king change.
5. **Long documents** are 9 % of the score, and kings seem to train on 2 048-token windows: the TEXT long-row pool
   gave +0.0053/token on documents. Put long rows into the MATH/CODE pools too.
6. **Submission readiness**: keep 2–3 funded, registered ed25519 hotkeys ready (registration burn ≈ 1.04 TAO), so a
   confirmed winner is submitted minutes after staging, before the next king moves the bar.
7. **Housekeeping**: rotate the HF and GitHub tokens (they were pasted into a chat); delete the 5 TB of incompatible
   old-king downloads (`/data/teutonic/search/ingest_failed.json` lists them).
